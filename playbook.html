Playbook

My expansion playbook

A repeatable system for expanding operations in any industry, built from scratch and used on every project, from the first charter to the final handover.
01

The five stages

01
Initiate
What I do
Develop the business case and charter with the sponsor. Identify stakeholders and map them on a power/interest grid.
What I produce
Business case, project charter, stakeholder register.
Real example
Cheese plant: agreed the charter with the operations director, setting target capacity, a budget ceiling and success criteria before any equipment was ordered.
02
Plan
What I do
Collect requirements. Define scope, WBS and scope baseline. Plan resources, procurement and risk. Integrate schedule and cost baselines.
What I produce
Scope statement, WBS, schedule and cost baselines, risk register, procurement plan.
Real example
Cheese plant: split the work into about 30 work packages and ordered the longest-lead equipment first, so installation never waited on deliveries.
03
Execute
What I do
Direct the work with engineers, technicians, vendors and contractors. Acquire resources. Manage quality and stakeholder engagement.
What I produce
Deliverables, vendor and contractor coordination, quality records.
Real example
Food-grade installations: pasteurization, pressing and brining, high-pressure pumps, heat exchangers, nitrogen injection, automated dosing, PLC-integrated packaging.
04
Control
What I do
Track schedule and cost against baselines. Run change requests through the CCB. Watch the risk register and trigger contingency plans.
What I produce
Variance analysis, change log, updated risk register, status reports.
Real example
Shortening line: a 3-week valve delay threatened the critical path. The contingency plan was triggered and the budget held.
05
Close
What I do
Get sponsor acceptance. Transfer the finished plant to operations. Document lessons learned. Archive records and release resources.
What I produce
Acceptance sign-off, handover pack, lessons-learned log.
Real example
Sauces line: handed over with trained operators, a spares list and a signed acceptance, then used the lessons-learned session to tighten vendor checks on the next project.
02

Stage gates

The project only moves forward when the gate is passed. This keeps the expansion project well planned
Gate 1
Charter approved
Business case, sponsor and success criteria agreed.
Gate 2
Baselines approved
Scope, schedule, budget and risks signed off.
Gate 3
Ready to install
Site, utilities and equipment are ready.
Gate 4
Ready to hand over
Quality checked, tests passed and operators trained.
03

My first 30 days with a new company

Week 1
Listen and map
Learn the operation, meet stakeholders, list current projects.
Week 2
Find the gaps
Review plans, risks and how work is tracked today.
Week 3
Set the basics
Charter, baselines and risk register for the priority project.
Week 4
Show progress
First status report to the sponsor and an expansion roadmap.
