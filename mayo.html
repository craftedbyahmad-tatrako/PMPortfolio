Mayonnaise Production Line

Launched a new mayonnaise line, with new machines from oil handling and cookers to packaging, opening new customers and new revenue.
Project
Mayonnaise Production Line
Context
Food manufacturing plant expansion, Jeddah
My role
Associate Project Manager
Tools
Primavera P6, MS Project, Excel, EVM
Result
New line, new customers, new revenue
01

Overview and problem

What it was
A new mayonnaise production line with new machines, from oil handling and cookers through to filling and packaging.
The problem
Add a new product line quickly, win new customers, and keep existing production running.
02

Goals and success criteria

Capability
Add a new mayonnaise product line.
Cost
Finish within the approved budget.
Quality
Meet food-grade standards at start-up.
Handover
Transfer to operations with trained operators.
03

My role and the team

I owned
Charter, scope and WBS, baselines, risk register, vendor coordination, change control, handover and lessons learned, plus monitoring and controlling execution.
I contributed to
Equipment selection, led by engineers.
Others handled
Equipment manufacturing and installation by vendors, contractors and technicians.
Stakeholders
Sponsor, sales, operations, engineers, technicians, quality team, vendors.
04

Constraints

Production could not stop for the installation.
Long lead times on imported equipment.
Several equipment suppliers to coordinate.
Food-grade quality requirements.
05

Process

1
Initiate
Agreed the charter and success criteria with the sponsor and sales, tying the line to new customer demand. Mapped stakeholders on a power/interest grid.
Output
Charter, stakeholder map
2
Plan
Defined scope from oil handling to cookers, mixing, filling and packaging. Built the WBS and baselines, and planned procurement around the longest-lead machines.
Output
Scope statement, WBS, baselines, risk register
3
Execute
Directed vendors and contractors through delivery, installation and integration, keeping the work away from live production areas.
Output
Installed and integrated line
4
Control
Tracked schedule and cost against baselines, ran changes through the CCB and watched risks to the start-up date.
Output
Variance reports, change log
5
Close
Validated deliverables, obtained sponsor acceptance, transferred to operations and recorded lessons learned.
Output
Acceptance, handover pack, lessons log
06

Artifacts and evidence

Why it mattered: it showed vendors and operations where each machine sat in the flow, so interfaces were agreed before installation.
07

Key decision

The situation
The line needed equipment from several suppliers, and each handover between machines was a risk.
What I did
Chose one main supplier for the core process and a separate supplier for packaging, with a named owner for each interface.
Options and trade-offs
(1) One supplier for the entire line: simplest interfaces, less choice. (2) A different supplier per section: best choice, many interfaces. (3) One core supplier plus separate packaging. I chose (3) as the best balance of control and flexibility.
08

Outcome

New line
added to the plant
New customers
a new product to sell
New revenue
more revenue for the company
Why this matters beyond food
Adding a product line next to live production is common in every sector: the work has to fit around operations that cannot stop.
09

Reflection

What I expected
I expected the machines to be the hardest part.
What happened
The hardest part was fitting the work around live production.
Why the gap existed
I had planned the installation area as if it were empty.
What I would change
I now agree working windows with operations before the plan is baselined.
