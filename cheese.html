New Cheese Plant

Delivered a new cheese plant from the ground up, with new machines and lines from oil handling and cookers to packaging, unlocking new customers and new revenue.
Project
New Cheese Plant
Context
Food manufacturing plant expansion, Jeddah
My role
Associate Project Manager
Tools
Primavera P6, MS Project, Excel, EVM
Result
New plant, new customers, new revenue
01

Overview and problem

What it was
A new cheese plant added to the factory: a new production area plus new machines and lines, from oil handling and cookers through to packaging and supporting systems.
The problem
Build a complete new plant with many vendors and interfaces, and start production on time without disturbing the running plant.
02

Goals and success criteria

Capability
Add a full cheese production capability.
Cost
Finish within the approved budget.
Quality
Meet food-grade requirements from day one.
Handover
Hand over a running plant to operations.
03

My role and the team

I owned
Charter, scope and WBS, schedule and cost baselines, risk register, procurement plan, vendor and contractor coordination, change control, handover and lessons learned. I also controlled and monitored execution across every work area.
I contributed to
Process and equipment decisions, led by engineers and technicians.
Others handled
Equipment manufacturing, area finishing and installation work by vendors, contractors, engineers and technicians.
Stakeholders
Sponsor, operations, engineers, technicians, quality team, vendors, contractors.
04

Constraints

A running plant next to the new area.
Many vendors and equipment interfaces.
Imported equipment with long lead times.
Food-grade quality requirements.
05

Process

1
Initiate
Built the business case for entering the cheese market and agreed the charter with the sponsor. Mapped operations, quality, engineering, vendors and contractors on a power/interest grid.
Output
Charter, stakeholder map
2
Plan
Defined the scope as a complete plant: area preparation, oil handling, cookers, pasteurization, pressing, brining, packaging and utilities. Broke the WBS into about 30 work packages, ordered the longest-lead equipment first and set baselines against the approved budget.
Output
Scope statement, WBS, baselines, risk register, procurement plan
3
Execute
Coordinated area preparation, equipment placement, utility connections and line integration across several vendors and contractors, with every interface owned by one named person.
Output
Installed and integrated lines
4
Control
Tracked progress and cost against both baselines, ran variance analysis, handled interface changes through the CCB and watched the risk register for long-lead items.
Output
Variance reports, change log, risk updates
5
Close
Validated deliverables, obtained sponsor acceptance, transferred the plant to operations with trained operators, recorded lessons learned and released resources.
Output
Acceptance, handover pack, lessons log
06

Artifacts and evidence

Why it mattered: the layout showed vendors and operations how product flows through the plant, so every interface was agreed before installation.
07

Key decision

The situation
Area preparation and equipment delivery could overlap, but starting both at once risked installing into an unfinished area.
What I did
Ran area preparation and long-lead ordering in parallel, and gated installation on a “ready to install” check.
Options and trade-offs
(1) Do everything in sequence: safest but slowest. (2) Run everything in parallel and accept the risk. (3) Run preparation and ordering in parallel with an installation gate. I chose (3) because it saved time without installing into an unready area.
08

Outcome

New plant
built from the ground up
New customers
a new product line to sell
New revenue
a new revenue stream for the company
Why this matters
A new multi-line site means many vendors, one critical path and a plant that must start on time. That is the same challenge in a pharma, packaging or logistics expansion.
09

Reflection

What I expected
I expected the interfaces between vendors to be the main risk.
What happened
Most delays came from waiting for the area, not the equipment.
Why the gap existed
I had planned the area as a simple task, not as a dependency of every installation.
What I would change
I now treat site readiness as a gated deliverable with its own owner and checklist.
