Case study · Expansion project

Shortening Production Line

Expanded production by 30% and stayed within budget, even after a 3-week valve delay hit the critical path.
Project
Shortening Production Line, UFVO
Context
Food manufacturing plant expansion, Jeddah
My role
Associate Project Manager
Tools
Primavera P6, MS Project, Excel, EVM
Result
+30% production, within budget
01

Overview and problem

What it was
A new shortening production line added during the plant’s expansion from 2 to 7 lines, with food-grade process and packaging equipment.
The problem
Increase production capacity without exceeding the approved budget, while equipment deliveries were not fully in my control.
02

Goals and success criteria

Capacity
Raise production through the new line.
Cost
Finish within the approved budget.
Schedule
Protect the critical path to start-up.
Handover
Transfer to operations with acceptance.
03

My role and the team

I owned
Charter, scope and WBS, schedule and cost baselines, risk register, vendor coordination, handover and lessons learned. I also controlled and monitored execution: tracked progress against the baselines, ran variance analysis, managed change control and watched risks throughout.
I contributed to
Technical and equipment decisions, led by engineers and technicians.
Others handled
Equipment manufacturing and installation work by engineers, technicians vendors and contractors.
Stakeholders
Sponsor, engineers, technicians, vendors, contractors, operations. Team of 40+ people.
04

Constraints

Fixed approved budget.
Imported equipment with long lead times.
First dedicated PM, so no existing PM process to reuse.
Food-grade quality requirements.
05

Process

1
Initiate
Built the business case and charter with the sponsor for the line’s capacity expansion. Mapped engineers, technicians, vendors, contractors and operations on a power/interest grid to plan how to engage each.
Output
Charter, stakeholder map
2
Plan
Defined the scope for the new line: process equipment (high-pressure pumps, scraped-surface heat exchangers, nitrogen injection), utilities and PLC-integrated packaging. Broke the WBS into procurement, installation, commissioning and handover. Set the schedule baseline with the valve as a critical-path item, and the cost baseline against the approved budget.
Output
Scope statement, WBS, schedule and cost baselines, risk register
3
Execute
Directed vendors, contractors, engineers and technicians through equipment delivery, installation and commissioning, and kept the food-grade quality requirements on track.
Output
Installed and commissioned line
4
Control
Tracked actual progress and cost against both baselines and ran variance analysis. When the valve slipped 3 weeks, I triggered the contingency plan, passed the resulting changes through the CCB, and kept cost within budget.
Output
Variance reports, change log, updated risk register
5
Close
Validated the deliverables, obtained sponsor acceptance, transferred the line to operations, recorded lessons learned, archived records and released resources.
Output
Acceptance, handover, lessons log
06

Artifacts and evidence

Why it mattered: the valve sat on the critical path. This view let me show the sponsor the 3-week impact and the recovery plan on one page.
07

Key decision

The situation
A valve delivery slipped by 3 weeks and threatened the critical path.
What I did
Triggered the contingency plan from the risk register instead of absorbing the delay.
Options and trade-offs
(1) Accept the delay and move the start-up date. (2) Pay for faster shipping. (3) Re-sequence the installation and trigger the contingency plan. I chose (3) because it protected both the date and the budget.
08

Outcome

+30%
production increase
On budget
despite the 3-week delay
Handed over
to operations with sponsor acceptance
Why this matters
Long-lead equipment, a fixed budget and a firm start-up date appear in every expansion, from a pharma packaging line to a chemical plant. The method I used here is the same in any of them.
09

Reflection

What I expected
I expected the valve to arrive on the planned date and the installation to run in a straight line.
What happened
The valve arrived 3 weeks late and pushed the whole critical path.
Why the gap existed
It was a long-lead item from a single vendor, and I had tracked it as routine, not as a critical-path risk.
What I would change
I now flag long-lead items as top risks from day one, track them weekly and line up a second source before ordering.
