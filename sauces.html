Sauces Packaging Line

Raised sauces production by 20% with a new automated packaging line for five sauces in cans and jars.
Project
Sauces Packaging Line
Context
Food manufacturing plant expansion, Jeddah
My role
Associate Project Manager
Tools
Primavera P6, MS Project, Excel, EVM
Result
+20% production
01

Overview and problem

What it was
A new automated packaging line for cheddar sauce, ketchup, BBQ, thousand island and ranch, packed in cans and jars.
The problem
Raise sauces output by 20% while handling five sauces and two container types on one line.
02

Goals and success criteria

Capacity
Raise sauces production by 20%.
Cost
Finish within the approved budget.
Flexibility
Run five sauces in cans and jars.
Handover
Transfer to operations with trained operators.
03

My role and the team

I owned
Charter, scope and WBS, baselines, risk register, vendor coordination, change control, handover and lessons learned, plus monitoring and controlling execution.
I contributed to
Equipment layout and line integration, led by engineers.
Others handled
Machine manufacturing, PLC programming and installation by vendors, contractors and technicians.
Stakeholders
Sponsor, production, quality team, engineers, technicians, vendors.
04

Constraints

Five sauces with different thicknesses.
Two container types on one line.
Short window for installation.
Food-grade quality requirements.
05

Process

1
Initiate
Agreed the charter and the 20% target with the sponsor. Mapped production, quality and vendors on a power/interest grid.
Output
Charter, stakeholder map
2
Plan
Defined scope: automated dosing, PLC-integrated filling, can and jar handling, conveyors and utilities. Built the WBS and baselines and planned changeovers with production.
Output
Scope statement, WBS, baselines, risk register
3
Execute
Directed vendors and contractors through installation, PLC integration and commissioning for both containers.
Output
Installed and commissioned line
4
Control
Tracked progress and cost against baselines, ran changes through the CCB and monitored risks to the start-up date.
Output
Variance reports, change log
5
Close
Validated output against the target, obtained sponsor acceptance, trained operators and recorded lessons learned.
Output
Acceptance, handover pack, lessons log
06

Artifacts and evidence

Why it mattered: it turned the 20% goal into a single visible target for the sponsor, the vendors and production.
07

Key decision

The situation
One line had to handle five sauces and both cans and jars, and a dedicated line per product was too costly.
What I did
Chose one flexible line with planned changeovers and a quick cleaning routine between sauces.
Options and trade-offs
(1) A dedicated line per sauce: highest output, highest cost and space. (2) One flexible line with planned changeovers. (3) Separate can and jar lines. I chose (2) as it hit the 20% target within the budget.
08

Outcome

+20%
production on the sauces line
5 sauces
on one flexible line
2 formats
cans and jars
Why this matters
A flexible line that handles several products and formats is the same design problem in beverages, pharma packaging and consumer goods.
09

Reflection

What I expected
I expected output to depend mainly on machine speed.
What happened
Changeover time between sauces had the biggest effect on output.
Why the gap existed
I had treated changeovers as an operations detail, not a design input.
What I would change
I now ask for changeover targets in the first requirements workshop.
